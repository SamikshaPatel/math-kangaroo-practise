# Math Kangaroo Benjamin Level Topic Taxonomy
## Complete Classification from 2009-2025 Past Papers

**Data Source:** Analysis of 15 official papers (2009-2025)  
**Levels Analyzed:** Benjamin (Grades 5-6) and Ecolier (Grades 3-4)  
**Total Questions Reviewed:** 360+ questions  
**Compiled:** September 2026

---

## **PART 1: CORE TOPIC CATEGORIES**

### **1. ARITHMETIC & NUMBER OPERATIONS**

#### 1.1 Basic Arithmetic (Multi-step)
- **Difficulty:** 3-4 points
- **Frequency:** Very High (appears in ~60% of papers)
- **Question Types:**
  - Multi-step calculation (addition, subtraction, multiplication, division)
  - Mixed operations with order of operations
  - Calculations with parentheses
  - Left-to-right vs correct order application
- **Key Skills:** PEMDAS/BODMAS mastery, mental math, avoiding calculation errors
- **Common Variations:**
  - Large numbers (3-4 digit operands)
  - Decimals mixed with whole numbers
  - Fractions in mixed form
  - Negative numbers (subtraction resulting in negatives)
- **Example Patterns:** "Calculate 345 + 267 - 189" or "If you multiply 12 by 8, then divide by 4..."

#### 1.2 Number Properties & Patterns
- **Difficulty:** 3-4 points (sometimes 5 for complex patterns)
- **Frequency:** High (35-40% of papers)
- **Question Types:**
  - Digit manipulation (sum of digits, digit properties)
  - Divisibility rules and factors
  - Prime numbers and composite numbers
  - Multiples and LCM
  - GCD problems
  - Perfect squares and cubes
  - Sequences and number series
- **Key Skills:** Pattern recognition, algebraic thinking, number theory fundamentals
- **Common Variations:**
  - "How many two-digit numbers are divisible by 5?"
  - "Find the next number in sequence: 2, 5, 10, 17, 26, ..."
  - "What is the GCD of 48 and 60?"
  - "Find a number where digits sum to X and divide evenly by Y"
- **Difficulty Progression:**
  - 3-pt: Simple Fibonacci-like sequences, basic divisibility
  - 4-pt: Digit sums, multiple conditions (divisible by AND)
  - 5-pt: Complex sequences, nested patterns, reverse-engineering numbers

#### 1.3 Percentages & Ratios
- **Difficulty:** 3-5 points
- **Frequency:** High (30-35%)
- **Question Types:**
  - Percentage calculations (finding % of a number)
  - Percentage increase/decrease
  - Ratio comparison and scaling
  - Proportion problems
  - Part-to-whole relationships
- **Key Skills:** Proportional reasoning, cross-multiplication, percentage conversions
- **Common Variations:**
  - "25% of 80 = ?"
  - "If 60% play basketball and there are 25 students, how many play basketball?"
  - "Boys to girls ratio is 3:2, if 12 boys, how many girls?"
  - "A price increases by 20%, then decreases by 10%. Net change?"
- **Difficulty Progression:**
  - 3-pt: Straightforward %age calculations (25%, 50%, etc.)
  - 4-pt: Word problems with one percentage operation
  - 5-pt: Multiple percentage operations or complex ratio scenarios

#### 1.4 Fractions & Decimal Operations
- **Difficulty:** 3-5 points
- **Frequency:** Very High (50%+)
- **Question Types:**
  - Fraction multiplication and division
  - Adding/subtracting fractions with unlike denominators
  - Simplifying fractions
  - Comparing fraction size
  - Fraction of a fraction
  - Mixed numbers conversions
  - Decimal-fraction equivalence
- **Key Skills:** Fraction manipulation, common denominators, simplification
- **Common Variations:**
  - "What is 1/2 of 3/4?"
  - "Which is larger: 3/7 or 2/5?"
  - "Convert 0.375 to simplest fraction form"
  - "Add 1/3 + 1/4 + 1/6"
- **Difficulty Progression:**
  - 3-pt: Simple multiplication (1/2 × 1/4), basic addition with common denominators
  - 4-pt: Unlike denominators, word problems with fractions
  - 5-pt: Chain operations, fractional word problems with multiple steps

### **2. GEOMETRY**

#### 2.1 2D Shapes - Perimeter & Area
- **Difficulty:** 3-5 points
- **Frequency:** Very High (55%+)
- **Question Types:**
  - Rectangle/square perimeter calculation
  - Rectangle/square area calculation
  - Triangle area (given base and height)
  - Finding missing dimension (given area/perimeter)
  - Composite shapes (area of L-shapes, T-shapes, etc.)
  - Irregular shapes (grids, grid counting)
  - Circle circumference and area (with π approximation)
- **Key Skills:** Formula application, spatial reasoning, unit consistency
- **Common Variations:**
  - "Rectangle is 24m long, 18m wide. Perimeter?" (straightforward)
  - "Square perimeter is 36cm. What is area?" (two-step)
  - "A cross made of unit squares, total area?" (counting/composite)
  - "A square with corners cut off. Area removed = 20cm², remaining = 16cm². Find perimeter of cross"
- **Difficulty Progression:**
  - 3-pt: Single formula application (L × W for rectangle)
  - 4-pt: Finding missing dimension, composite shapes
  - 5-pt: Backwards problems (find dimension from area), complex composites

#### 2.2 3D Shapes - Volume & Surface Area
- **Difficulty:** 4-5 points
- **Frequency:** Medium-High (25-30%)
- **Question Types:**
  - Cube volume (side³)
  - Rectangular prism volume (L × W × H)
  - Volume word problems
  - Surface area of cubes and prisms
  - Stacking problems (multiple cubes/blocks)
  - Counting visible cubes in 3D arrangements
- **Key Skills:** 3D visualization, formula application, mental rotation
- **Common Variations:**
  - "Cube with 5cm side. Volume?" (straightforward)
  - "Rectangular prism 8×6×4. Volume? Surface area?"
  - "Stack of unit cubes arranged in pattern. How many visible from outside?"
  - "How many 1cm cubes fit in a 10cm × 10cm × 10cm box?"
- **Difficulty Progression:**
  - 3-pt: Simple cube volume (given side length)
  - 4-pt: Rectangular prism, composite 3D shapes
  - 5-pt: Surface area, counting problems, complex stacking arrangements

#### 2.3 Angles & Triangles
- **Difficulty:** 3-5 points
- **Frequency:** Medium (20-25%)
- **Question Types:**
  - Angle sum in triangles (180°)
  - Finding missing angle
  - Properties of special triangles (isosceles, equilateral)
  - Angles on a straight line (180°)
  - Angles around a point (360°)
  - Complementary and supplementary angles
  - Angle bisectors
- **Key Skills:** Understanding angle relationships, working with angle properties
- **Common Variations:**
  - "Triangle has angles 50°, 65°, x°. Find x"
  - "If two angles of isosceles triangle are equal, and one is 70°, find others"
  - "Straight line has three angles. If two are 60° and 80°, find third"
- **Difficulty Progression:**
  - 3-pt: Simple angle sum (180° in triangle)
  - 4-pt: Multiple angle properties combined
  - 5-pt: Complex angle relationships, proofs

#### 2.4 Symmetry & Transformations
- **Difficulty:** 3-5 points
- **Frequency:** Medium-High (30-35%)
- **Question Types:**
  - Line symmetry identification
  - Rotational symmetry
  - 90°/180° rotations
  - Reflections (mirror images)
  - Tessellations
  - Transformations (translate, rotate, reflect)
  - Symmetry in patterns
- **Key Skills:** Spatial reasoning, mental rotation, pattern recognition
- **Common Variations:**
  - "How many lines of symmetry does a square have? Rectangle? Triangle?"
  - "Rotate 45° clockwise. After how many rotations returns to start? (360÷45=8)"
  - "Which shape is the mirror image?"
  - "Can this shape tile a plane?"
- **Difficulty Progression:**
  - 3-pt: Identifying lines of symmetry in common shapes
  - 4-pt: Rotations, reflections, multiple transformation steps
  - 5-pt: Complex rotation sequences, tessellation problems

#### 2.5 Coordinate Geometry & Grids
- **Difficulty:** 4-5 points
- **Frequency:** Low-Medium (15-20%)
- **Question Types:**
  - Points on grids/coordinate planes
  - Distance between points
  - Finding coordinates
  - Grid-based area (Pick's theorem context, though not explicitly named)
  - Path counting on grids
- **Key Skills:** Coordinate understanding, distance formula awareness
- **Common Variations:**
  - "Point A at (2,3), Point B at (5,7). Distance?"
  - "How many shortest paths from (0,0) to (3,3) moving right/up only?"
  - "Area enclosed by these coordinates?"
- **Difficulty Progression:**
  - 4-pt: Simple distance, basic coordinates
  - 5-pt: Path counting, area calculation

### **3. LOGIC & DEDUCTION**

#### 3.1 Logic Puzzles & Truth-Teller Problems
- **Difficulty:** 4-5 points
- **Frequency:** Medium (25-30%)
- **Question Types:**
  - Truth-teller vs liar scenarios
  - Days of week logic (who tells truth which days)
  - Constraint satisfaction (A beats B, B beats C, therefore...)
  - Multi-person reasoning
  - Property assignment (matching attributes to people/items)
- **Key Skills:** Logical deduction, systematic elimination, constraint tracking
- **Common Variations:**
  - "Klaus never tells truth on Mon/Wed/Fri. He says 'Today is Tuesday' and 'Tomorrow is Thursday.' What day is it?"
  - "Five athletes finished race: Sarah 2nd, Tom 5th, Maria between them, Alex beat Tom but not Sarah, Nina just ahead of Sarah. Order?"
  - "Three people: one always lies, one always tells truth, one sometimes tells truth. Who is who?"
- **Difficulty Progression:**
  - 4-pt: Simple truth-teller (one statement per person)
  - 5-pt: Complex multi-statement logic, race ordering, property matching

#### 3.2 Logical Reasoning & Deduction
- **Difficulty:** 3-4 points
- **Frequency:** Medium (20%)
- **Question Types:**
  - "If-then" logic
  - Missing number/digit problems
  - Hidden digit inference (rain washed away digits, figure out what they were)
  - Price ordering with constraints
- **Key Skills:** Constraint inference, systematic checking
- **Common Variations:**
  - "Menu shows prices but rain washed digits: Item 1: 3.5_, Item 2: _.75, Item 3: 5.4_. Items in price order. Item 2 valid price?"
  - "Three cards show numbers with missing digits. Find which card has matching digit sums"
- **Difficulty Progression:**
  - 3-pt: Simple constraint problems
  - 4-pt: Multiple constraints, digit problems

#### 3.3 Grid-Based Logic (Sudoku-like)
- **Difficulty:** 4-5 points
- **Frequency:** Low-Medium (10-15%)
- **Question Types:**
  - Fill grid with numbers (0s and 1s, 1-9, etc.)
  - Row/column sum constraints
  - Diagonal sum constraints
  - No-repeat rules
- **Key Skills:** Constraint satisfaction, systematic solving
- **Common Variations:**
  - "Fill 3×3 grid with 0s and 1s so each row, column, diagonal sums to 3"
  - "Each row must have numbers 1-5 exactly once, columns must have certain sums"
- **Difficulty Progression:**
  - 4-pt: Simple grids with 2-3 constraints
  - 5-pt: Complex multi-constraint grids

#### 3.4 Graph/Network Problems
- **Difficulty:** 5 points
- **Frequency:** Low (5-10%)
- **Question Types:**
  - Connection/path problems
  - Handshake problems (who talked to whom)
  - Network connectivity
- **Key Skills:** Graph visualization, relationship tracking
- **Common Variations:**
  - "In a group, some people know each other. If A knows B, B knows C, who knows whom overall?"

### **4. PATTERNS & SEQUENCES**

#### 4.1 Number Sequences
- **Difficulty:** 3-5 points
- **Frequency:** High (40-45%)
- **Question Types:**
  - Arithmetic sequences (constant difference)
  - Geometric sequences (constant ratio)
  - Fibonacci-like sequences (each term sum of previous two)
  - Squared/cubed sequences
  - Factorial sequences
  - Mixed operation sequences
  - Digit-based sequences
- **Key Skills:** Pattern recognition, extrapolation, rule identification
- **Common Variations:**
  - "2, 5, 10, 17, 26, _" (differences: 3, 5, 7, 9 → next difference 11 → 37)
  - "1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, _, _" (Fibonacci → 13, 21)
  - "1, 4, 9, 16, 25, _" (perfect squares → 36)
  - "Pattern increases by multiplying by 2: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, _" (→ 32)
- **Difficulty Progression:**
  - 3-pt: Arithmetic sequences, obvious patterns
  - 4-pt: Fibonacci-like, squared sequences, more complex differences
  - 5-pt: Mixed operations, digit manipulation, reverse-engineering sequences

#### 4.2 Visual/Geometric Patterns
- **Difficulty:** 3-5 points
- **Frequency:** High (35-40%)
- **Question Types:**
  - Shape patterns (growing number of shapes)
  - Hexagon arrangements, triangle arrangements
  - Grid patterns (shading, fill patterns)
  - Increasing/decreasing patterns
  - Symmetrical pattern continuation
- **Key Skills:** Visual analysis, spatial reasoning, counting
- **Common Variations:**
  - "Pattern shows hexagons growing: [5] → [8] → [?]. Complete the pattern"
  - "Cross-shaped arrangement of dots grows. How many dots after N iterations?"
  - "Checkerboard pattern with alternating fill. Continue the pattern"
- **Difficulty Progression:**
  - 3-pt: Obvious shape addition (one more triangle each time)
  - 4-pt: Geometric growth patterns, complex arrangements
  - 5-pt: Multi-level patterns, reverse patterns

#### 4.3 Transformation & Folding Patterns
- **Difficulty:** 4-5 points
- **Frequency:** Medium (20-25%)
- **Question Types:**
  - Paper folding and unfolding
  - Net-to-3D conversions
  - Cross patterns folding into cube
  - Resulting shape after folds
- **Key Skills:** 3D visualization, mental unfolding, spatial reasoning
- **Common Variations:**
  - "Cross-shaped net with 5 squares. When folded, which square is opposite the center?"
  - "Paper folded twice then has hole punched. How many holes when unfolded?"
  - "Which net folds into this cube?"
- **Difficulty Progression:**
  - 4-pt: Simple cube nets, single folds
  - 5-pt: Multiple folds, complex unfolding

### **5. COMBINATORICS & COUNTING**

#### 5.1 Systematic Counting
- **Difficulty:** 4-5 points
- **Frequency:** Medium (25-30%)
- **Question Types:**
  - Arrangements/permutations (in order)
  - Combinations (selections)
  - Counting with constraints
  - Counting paths
  - Counting valid arrangements
- **Key Skills:** Systematic enumeration, avoiding duplicates, constraint satisfaction
- **Common Variations:**
  - "How many ways to arrange 5 people in a line?"
  - "How many different bouquets for exactly €25: roses €3, tulips €4, sunflowers €5?"
  - "In a 4×5 grid, how many shortest paths from top-left to bottom-right (only right/down)?"
  - "How many 2-digit numbers can be formed from {1,2,3,4,5}?"
- **Difficulty Progression:**
  - 4-pt: Simple counting with one constraint
  - 5-pt: Multiple constraints, complex enumeration

#### 5.2 Geometric Counting
- **Difficulty:** 3-5 points
- **Frequency:** Medium-High (30-35%)
- **Question Types:**
  - Counting shapes in a figure (triangles, squares, etc.)
  - Counting line segments
  - Counting regions/areas
  - Counting cubes in 3D arrangements
  - Counting intersection points
- **Key Skills:** Systematic visual scanning, avoiding double-counting
- **Common Variations:**
  - "How many triangles are in this figure?"
  - "Lattice of points 5×5. How many line segments between adjacent points?"
  - "Stack of cubes arranged 3×3×3. How many visible from outside?"
  - "Lines crossing to form regions. How many regions total?"
- **Difficulty Progression:**
  - 3-pt: Simple triangle counting in obvious arrangements
  - 4-pt: Multiple shape types, overlapping regions
  - 5-pt: Complex 3D cube stacking, nested shapes

#### 5.3 Probability & Chance
- **Difficulty:** 4-5 points
- **Frequency:** Medium (20-25%)
- **Question Types:**
  - Probability calculation (favorable/total outcomes)
  - Spinner probabilities
  - Dice roll probabilities
  - Card drawing probabilities
  - Comparing probabilities
- **Key Skills:** Fraction understanding, proportional reasoning
- **Common Variations:**
  - "Spinner 1: 8 parts, 2 black | Spinner 2: 6 parts, 2 black | Spinner 3: 5 parts, 2 black | Which gives best chance of black?"
  - "Probability of rolling two dice and getting sum > 10?"
  - "From deck of cards, probability of drawing red card?"
- **Difficulty Progression:**
  - 4-pt: Simple probability calculation, single event
  - 5-pt: Comparing probabilities, multiple events

## **PART 2: DIFFICULTY PROGRESSION & POINT DISTRIBUTION**

### **By Point Value**

#### **3-Point Questions (Easier - ~40% of test)**
- **Characteristics:** Straightforward, one-step or two-step problems
- **Most Common Topics:**
  1. Basic arithmetic (+, −, ×, ÷)
  2. Simple perimeter/area calculations
  3. Basic pattern recognition
  4. Fraction/percentage basics
  5. Angle sums (180° in triangle)
  6. Simple geometry counting
  7. Elementary logic puzzles
- **Time Budget:** ~90 seconds per question (ideal 60-min test)
- **Strategy:** Solve quickly, double-check, move on. Should be high success rate (90%+)

#### **4-Point Questions (Medium - ~30% of test)**
- **Characteristics:** Multi-step, require some visualization or constraint juggling
- **Most Common Topics:**
  1. Area/perimeter with missing dimensions
  2. 3D volume and surface area
  3. Speed/distance/time calculations
  4. Probability comparisons
  5. Moderate pattern recognition
  6. Logic puzzles with 2-3 constraints
  7. Fractions with unlike denominators
  8. Ratio and proportion word problems
- **Time Budget:** ~2 minutes per question
- **Strategy:** Read carefully, work systematically, may need trial-and-error. 70-80% success rate realistic

#### **5-Point Questions (Harder - ~30% of test)**
- **Characteristics:** Complex visualization, multiple constraints, creative problem-solving
- **Most Common Topics:**
  1. Complex combinatorics/counting
  2. 3D folding and unfolding
  3. Advanced pattern recognition
  4. Truth-teller logic (multiple statements)
  5. Geometric counting (overlapping shapes)
  6. Complex transformation problems
  7. Constraint satisfaction with 3+ conditions
  8. Competitive/racing logic problems
  9. Tiling and tessellation
  10. 3D cube stacking scenarios
- **Time Budget:** ~3-4 minutes per question
- **Strategy:** Don't guess - blank is safer (0 pts) than wrong (-1.25 pts). 40-60% success rate realistic

## **PART 4: QUESTION DESIGN PATTERNS BY DIFFICULTY**

### **How 3-Point Questions Are Constructed**
1. **Single formula application** - Apply one concept directly
2. **Two-step with obvious steps** - Formula then simple calculation
3. **Pattern extension** - Obvious rule, find next
4. **Straightforward word problem** - Clear setup, standard solution
5. **Basic property application** - Use known rule (e.g., sum of angles = 180°)

**Distractor Pattern:** Wrong answers usually come from:
- Off-by-one errors
- Calculation mistakes
- Misapplying formula
- Common misconceptions (e.g., perimeter = area)

### **How 4-Point Questions Are Constructed**
1. **Two-concept combination** - Use two different rules together
2. **Reverse problem** - Given result, find input
3. **Constraint with multiple conditions** - Must satisfy 2-3 constraints
4. **Comparison** - Which one has most/least of property
5. **Moderate pattern** - Pattern with less obvious rule
6. **Multi-step word problem** - 3-4 calculation steps

**Distractor Pattern:** Wrong answers from:
- Partial solutions (got one constraint, missed another)
- Computational errors
- Misunderstanding constraints
- Premature rounding

### **How 5-Point Questions Are Constructed**
1. **Complex visualization** - Needs mental 3D rotation or unfolding
2. **Constraint satisfaction with optimization** - Multiple constraints plus finding best/worst
3. **Logical deduction chain** - Must chain multiple deductions
4. **Systematic enumeration** - Must list all possibilities and count carefully
5. **Reverse-engineering** - Given constraints, find the setup
6. **Multi-layer pattern** - Pattern that repeats at different scales

**Distractor Pattern:** Wrong answers from:
- Partial enumeration (missed some cases)
- Logic error partway through chain
- Misunderstanding 3D visualization
- Constraint satisfaction failure

## **PART 5: COMMON SUBTOPICS BY CATEGORY**

### **Arithmetic Subtopics That Appear Repeatedly**
- Order of operations with parentheses
- Negative number arithmetic
- Large number calculations (1000+)
- Mixed operations (+ − × ÷ in one problem)
- Digit manipulation (sum of digits, etc.)

### **Geometry Subtopics That Appear Repeatedly**
- Rectangle/square (most frequent shape)
- L-shaped and T-shaped composites
- Triangles (area with base × height ÷ 2)
- Circles (circumference with π ≈ 22/7)
- Cubes (volume = s³, distinct from rectangular prisms)
- Cross-shaped figures (especially in folding)

### **Logic Subtopics That Appear Repeatedly**
- Truth-teller/liar on days of week
- Race finish order with constraints
- Property matching (who has which trait)
- Hidden digit/number inference
